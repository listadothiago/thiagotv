Programme notes

Vanilla Ice - Ice Ice Baby (Official Music Video)

music // Aired 2026-07-01

Summary

A 1990 single built on the bassline from Queen and David Bowie's "Under Pressure", topped with a rapped vocal and a video of stylised street tableaux. It was the record that carried rap to the top of the American pop chart for the first time, and it did so by wrapping the form in something already familiar to a rock audience.

Musically it is thin by design: the borrowed bass figure carries almost everything, the drums are a plain programmed pattern, and the hooks arrive early and often. That efficiency is exactly why it worked on radio, and exactly why it has been used ever since as shorthand for pop's ability to absorb a subculture and sell it back simplified.

Why it matters

Its commercial success marked the point where American radio programmers stopped treating rap as a niche and started treating it as a format, which changed what got signed and promoted for the rest of the decade.

It is also a case study in how sampling was understood before the law caught up. The record arrived during the window when hip-hop's borrowing practices were colliding with copyright, and its fame made it the example everyone reached for.

Controversy

The bassline was used without initial credit or clearance, and the artist's early public defence — that the two figures differed by an added note — is remembered as an unusually flimsy piece of reasoning. The matter was settled, and Queen and Bowie were subsequently credited as writers.

Separately, elements of the biography presented in early promotion were disputed by reporting at the time, which damaged his standing with an audience that valued authenticity above almost everything.
