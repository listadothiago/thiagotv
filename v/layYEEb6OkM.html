Night of the living dead 1990 FULL MOVIE

films // Aired 2026-08-07

Summary

Tom Savini's 1990 remake of Night of the Living Dead, from a screenplay by George A. Romero, who wrote and directed the 1968 original. Savini had made his name as a special-effects and makeup artist on Dawn of the Dead and Day of the Dead, and this was his feature directing debut.\n\nThe remake follows the shape of the original closely — a group of strangers barricaded in a rural farmhouse while the dead walk outside — but changes its centre. Barbara, near-catatonic for most of the 1968 film, is rewritten here as the most clear-headed person in the house, and the film's arc belongs to her rather than to the men arguing over the cellar.\n\nIt was shot in colour, with the gore that Savini's reputation implied, though studio pressure over the rating trimmed a good deal of it.

Why it matters

The remake exists largely because of a legal accident. The 1968 original entered the public domain almost immediately after release, when its distributor changed the title and failed to carry over the copyright notice, and Romero and his collaborators never made the money the film's success should have produced. Remaking it was, in part, an attempt to recover ownership of their own work.\n\nAs a film it is one of the more thoughtful horror remakes, because it treats the original as a text to argue with rather than a property to reproduce. The rewriting of Barbara is a direct response to decades of criticism about the 1968 character, and the ending departs from the original in a way that changes what the story is about.\n\nSavini's move from makeup effects to direction also marks a moment when practical-effects artists were becoming authors in horror rather than technicians on it — a path several of his contemporaries followed.

Controversy

The copyright failure that put the 1968 film in the public domain is one of the most-cited accidents in film history: it is why that film is freely available everywhere, why it has been endlessly reissued and colourised by parties with no connection to its makers, and why its creators saw so little of its earnings. This remake is inseparable from that history.\n\nThe production itself was cut down under pressure from the ratings board, and Savini has spoken about the difference between the film he shot and the film released — a routine but consequential form of studio control over horror at the time.
