Geneva Karr & Hershii LiqCour-Jeté’s Ava Max Lip Sync 💘 RuPaul’s Drag Ra

drag // Aired 2026-07-01

Summary

Two queens, one Ava Max song, and a wig that would not survive the encounter. This is the Ball-challenge lip sync from the third episode of RuPaul's Drag Race season sixteen, and it put Geneva Karr against Hershii LiqCour-Jeté with the loser going home.

Geneva Karr came into the season from Brownsville, on the Texas–Mexico border, and brought the specific confidence of a queen who has been the biggest thing in a small scene for years. Hershii LiqCour-Jeté arrived from a very different direction — a seasoned performer who had sold her car to be able to afford being on the show, a detail she talked about openly afterwards and one that tells you exactly how much this stage was worth to her.

The song was "Maybe You're the Problem", and the two of them read it completely differently. That is what makes the pairing worth rewatching: it is not two people doing the same routine at different skill levels, it is two arguments about what a lip sync is for. Geneva took it. Hershii went home in third episode, and a substantial part of the audience thought the judges got it wrong — a view loud enough that it followed the episode around for weeks.

Why it matters

The lip sync is the one thing Drag Race invented that the rest of television has never managed to copy. It is a competition segment with no points and no criteria, decided entirely by whether someone convinced you, and it turns a reality-show elimination into a genuine performance form. Fourteen seasons in, it is also the format's most-watched export: these clips travel far beyond people who follow the show, which is why the franchise posts them individually.

This particular one sits inside season sixteen's most-argued twist. The season introduced Rate-a-Queen, where contestants ranked each other and those rankings fed into who was at risk. Hershii was blunt afterwards about hating it, and the mechanic was widely criticised for making eliminations about the room's politics rather than the week's work. Her exit is one of the moments people point to when making that case.

There is also a small piece of trivia that has become the standard way this lip sync gets referenced. Geneva went on to lose a later LaLaPaRuZa lip sync to Morphine Love Dion — to another Ava Max song, "Million Dollar Baby". Winning and losing to the same artist puts her in a tiny group of queens across the whole franchise, and it is the kind of detail the fandom archives obsessively.

Geneva's own run ended in the sixth episode, sent home after a lip sync against Mhi'ya Iman Le'Paige, one of the strongest lip syncers the season had. She left having been in the bottom and fought her way out of it more than once, which is a particular kind of Drag Race legacy: not the winner, but the queen the edit kept putting in danger because she was good in danger.

Controversy

Hershii's elimination is genuinely contested rather than routinely so. A meaningful share of viewers scored the lip sync for her, and the disagreement was amplified by the Rate-a-Queen twist that had put her in the bottom in the first place — a mechanic she publicly criticised, and which the show did not keep in later seasons.

It is worth being clear about what is and isn't disputed. Who won is a judging decision and RuPaul's call is the only one that counts; what is argued is whether the format around it was fair. That distinction is the whole reason this clip still gets posted with an argument attached.
